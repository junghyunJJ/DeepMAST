Comprehensive Analysis of Cell-Cell Interactions in Human Breast Cancer

Spatial Transcriptomics Analysis using Visium Technology - Focus on Invasive Tumor Regions

Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive cell-cell interaction (CCI) analysis of human breast cancer tissue using Visium spatial transcriptomics technology. The analysis systematically identified invasive tumor regions, characterized their molecular signatures, and uncovered critical ligand-receptor interactions driving tumor progression in the breast cancer microenvironment.

Key Findings:

  • Primary Tumor Clusters: Clusters 4, 17, and 14 show the highest enrichment of Invasive Tumor and Prolif Invasive Tumor cell types with exceptional statistical significance (FDR < 10-20)
  • Dominant Pathway Activation: MAPK pathway shows extreme hyperactivation (stat=47.86) in invasive regions, followed by PI3K (stat=33.05)
  • Hypoxia-Proliferation Axis: Strong hypoxia signature drives proliferative invasive tumor phenotype (MISTy importance=0.764)
  • p53 Pathway Activation: Elevated p53 signaling associated with invasive phenotypes (MISTy importance=0.474)
  • Spatial Organization: Invasive tumor clusters show preferential spatial co-localization with Cluster 6 (enrichment score=54.37)
  • CCI Pattern (Factor3): Identified dominant intercellular communication pattern (Factor3) enriched in all invasive tumor regions
  • Critical L-R Interactions: 19 significant ligand-receptor pairs identified, with CDH1^ERBB3 showing highest significance (Moran's R=0.477, Factor3=1.197)
  • Microenvironment Components: Perivascular-Like cells and T Cell & Tumor Hybrid populations co-localize with invasive tumor regions

This section presents the foundational spatial transcriptomics analysis identifying tumor regions, cell type compositions, spatial relationships, and pathway activities in the breast cancer microenvironment.

Figure 1: Spatial distribution of tissue clusters across the breast cancer sample. The tissue demonstrates heterogeneous spatial organization with distinct tumor, stromal, and immune-enriched regions.

Biological Interpretation:

The spatial clustering reveals distinct tumor architectures within the breast cancer tissue. This spatial heterogeneity is a hallmark characteristic of breast cancer and reflects the complex microenvironmental interactions that drive tumor progression. The tissue demonstrates clear delineation between different functional regions, each with unique molecular signatures and cellular compositions.

Spatial transcriptomics has revolutionized our understanding of tumor heterogeneity by enabling simultaneous measurement of gene expression and spatial location. This approach reveals how tumor cells organize themselves and interact with their surrounding microenvironment, providing insights that are impossible to obtain from traditional bulk sequencing methods (PMID: 39703753).

Detailed Biological Interpretation of Top 3 Interactions:

1. CDH1^ERBB3 (E-cadherin → HER3): This interaction represents the highest priority target with exceptional spatial co-localization (Moran's R=0.477) and Factor3 loading (1.197). CDH1 (E-cadherin) is a classical cell adhesion molecule whose loss is a hallmark of invasive lobular carcinoma and EMT. However, its presence and interaction with ERBB3 (HER3) in invasive tumor regions suggests a more complex role.

E-cadherin can interact with ERBB family receptors to modulate their signaling. Recent studies have shown that E-cadherin-HER3 interactions can promote tumor cell survival and proliferation in specific contexts. Desmocollin-1 (DSC1) pull-down experiments identified interactions with CDH1, CDH3, HER2, and HER3 in luminal A breast cancer (PMID: 37620794). The association with both MAPK and PI3K pathways indicates this interaction drives proliferative signaling. The spatial variable expression in Invasive Tumor, T Cell & Tumor Hybrid, and Myoepi ACTA2+ cells suggests this represents a critical communication axis in invasive regions (PMID: 36691073), (PMID: 34359596).

2. LRIG1^ERBB3: LRIG1 (Leucine Rich Repeats And Immunoglobulin Like Domains 1) is a negative regulator of EGFR family signaling. Its interaction with ERBB3 may represent a feedback regulation mechanism. The high Factor3 loading (1.172) and spatial co-localization (Moran's R=0.363) suggest this regulatory interaction is spatially organized in invasive regions. LRIG1 has been shown to suppress tumor growth by promoting ERBB receptor degradation, and its expression patterns may reflect ongoing attempts to regulate aberrant HER3 signaling in invasive tumor cells.

3. RET^PTPRF / VEGFA^RET: These interactions (Factor3=1.162 and 1.158) highlight the importance of RET proto-oncogene signaling in invasive tumor communication. VEGFA-RET interaction is particularly significant given the presence of Perivascular-Like cells in invasive regions. PTPRF (Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase Receptor Type F) involvement suggests regulation of phosphorylation-dependent signaling cascades. The receptor ctSVG showing enrichment in Invasive Tumor, Prolif Invasive Tumor, and Perivascular-Like cells indicates these interactions bridge tumor cells with vascular components.

Comprehensive Summary: Integrated Multi-Scale Analysis

Overall Biological Model

This comprehensive spatial transcriptomics analysis reveals a hierarchically organized breast cancer tumor microenvironment centered on invasive and proliferative tumor regions. Three spatially distinct but molecularly similar tumor clusters (4, 17, 14) form the core of the invasive tumor mass, characterized by extreme MAPK/PI3K pathway activation, hypoxia-driven proliferation, and organized intercellular communication networks.

Integrated Findings Across All Analyses

1. Spatial Architecture of Invasive Regions

The tumor demonstrates organized spatial structure rather than random distribution. Primary invasive region (Cluster 4) shows the strongest molecular signatures and serves as the probable origin of tumor spread. Secondary (Cluster 17) and tertiary (Cluster 14) invasive foci maintain similar molecular characteristics while showing spatial continuity. Cluster 6 forms a consistent supportive microenvironment around all invasive regions (enrichment scores: 54.37, 32.32, 28.29), likely containing stromal and immune components that facilitate tumor progression.

2. Convergent Oncogenic Signaling

MAPK Dominance: Extreme MAPK pathway hyperactivation (stat=47.86 in Cluster 4) represents the most powerful molecular signal across the entire dataset. This activation drives proliferation, invasion, and EMT.

PI3K Co-activation: Concurrent PI3K activation (stat=33.05) provides complementary survival and metabolic signals, creating redundant pro-tumorigenic pathways that enhance therapeutic resistance.

Hypoxia-Proliferation Axis: Hypoxia signaling shows the strongest predictive relationship with proliferative invasive tumor distribution (MISTy importance=0.764), revealing that proliferation paradoxically thrives in oxygen-limited conditions through metabolic adaptation.

p53 Pathway Complexity: Strong p53-invasion association (MISTy importance=0.474) suggests either stress-induced wild-type p53 activation or gain-of-function mutant p53 driving invasion.

3. Intercellular Communication Networks

Factor3 as Invasive Signature: Factor3 represents the dominant intercellular communication pattern specific to invasive tumor regions (scores: 0.739, 0.662, 0.625 in Clusters 4, 17, 14). This factor is enriched in hypoxia and TGFb-related interactions while depleted of estrogen-related signaling, suggesting a hormone-independent, hypoxia-adapted communication program.

Cadherin-ERBB3 Axis: Multiple cadherin-ERBB3 interactions (CDH1^ERBB3, CDH1^PTPRF, CDH1^CDH2) dominate the top-ranked ligand-receptor pairs, suggesting E-cadherin plays complex roles beyond simple cell adhesion. Rather than complete loss (as in classical EMT), maintained E-cadherin expression may enable specific signaling interactions with ERBB receptors that drive proliferation while maintaining some epithelial characteristics—consistent with partial EMT or epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity.

4. Tumor-Immune Landscape

T Cell & Tumor Hybrid populations are consistently enriched across all invasive clusters, suggesting active tumor-immune interfaces. However, these may represent immune evasion mechanisms rather than effective anti-tumor immunity. The presence of these hybrid states alongside proliferative tumor cells suggests ongoing immunoediting where tumor cells have acquired properties that enable immune escape while maintaining growth capacity (PMID: 39981232).

5. Vascular and Stromal Support

Perivascular-Like cell enrichment in hypoxic invasive regions (MISTy importance=0.574) indicates active angiogenic remodeling. The strong spatial associations between tumor and supportive clusters suggest organized construction of tumor-supportive niches through recruitment and reprogramming of stromal and vascular components.

Clinical and Therapeutic Implications

Pathway-Based Therapeutic Opportunities

  • Dual MAPK/PI3K Inhibition: The concurrent extreme activation of both pathways suggests combination therapy targeting both MAPK and PI3K may be more effective than single-agent approaches. The redundancy in these pathways likely contributes to therapeutic resistance.
  • Hypoxia Targeting: The central role of hypoxia in driving proliferative invasive phenotype suggests hypoxia-activated prodrugs or HIF-1α inhibitors may preferentially target the most aggressive tumor regions.
  • ERBB3-Targeted Therapies: The dominance of ERBB3-containing interactions (CDH1^ERBB3, LRIG1^ERBB3, EGF^ERBB3, TGFA^ERBB3) identifies HER3 as a high-priority therapeutic target. HER3-directed antibody-drug conjugates or tyrosine kinase inhibitors may disrupt multiple critical communication pathways simultaneously (PMID: 38441358).
  • Anti-Angiogenic Approaches: VEGF pathway activation and perivascular cell enrichment suggest anti-angiogenic therapy may disrupt vascular support for invasive regions.

Biomarker Potential

  • Spatial Biomarkers: Factor3 score distribution could serve as a spatial biomarker identifying invasive tumor regions with highest risk for progression.
  • Pathway Activation Signatures: MAPK/PI3K co-activation status may predict response to targeted therapies.
  • Hypoxia Signatures: Hypoxia-related gene expression patterns may identify patients who would benefit from hypoxia-targeted interventions.
  • CDH1-ERBB3 Axis: Expression and co-localization of E-cadherin and HER3 may serve as predictive biomarkers for HER3-targeted therapies.

Spatial Heterogeneity Considerations

The existence of multiple invasive tumor foci (Clusters 4, 17, 14) with similar but not identical molecular profiles emphasizes intratumoral heterogeneity. Effective therapy must account for this spatial heterogeneity, as treating one region may lead to expansion of molecularly distinct regions. Multi-region sampling or spatial profiling may be necessary for accurate biomarker assessment and treatment planning (PMID: 39703753).

Conclusions

This comprehensive spatial transcriptomics analysis reveals breast cancer as a spatially organized disease with hierarchical tumor regions, convergent oncogenic signaling, and organized intercellular communication networks. The extreme MAPK/PI3K activation, hypoxia-driven proliferation, and cadherin-ERBB3 signaling axis represent high-priority therapeutic targets. The identification of Factor3 as the invasive tumor communication signature provides a framework for understanding how tumor cells coordinate their behavior across space to achieve collective invasive behavior.

The spatial organization, rather than being random, reflects underlying biological principles where tumor cells create supportive microenvironmental niches, maintain communication networks through specific ligand-receptor interactions, and adapt to hypoxic stress through coordinated molecular programs.

  • Dual MAPK/PI3K pathway activation
  • Hypoxia-driven proliferation program
  • Factor3 intercellular communication network
