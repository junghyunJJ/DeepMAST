# DeepMAST: An Evidence-Governed Agent Harness for Literature-Grounded Interpretation and Therapeutic Target Prioritization in Spatial Transcriptomics

## Introduction

Spatial transcriptomics links gene expression to tissue architecture by preserving spatial coordinates during transcriptome-wide profiling of intact tissue sections [1]. This capability has transformed studies of tissue organization, development, and disease by enabling investigators to ask where molecular programs occur, how cellular neighborhoods are arranged, and how spatially localized signals relate to pathological context [2]. Integration with single-cell transcriptomics extends this framework to cell-state mapping, niche analysis, intercellular-communication inference, and tissue-dynamics reconstruction [3]. However, the same data richness that makes spatial transcriptomics powerful also creates a translation bottleneck. Spatial resolution, assay throughput, morphology, cell-type annotation, spatially variable gene detection, cell-cell interaction inference, multimodal integration, and analytical strategy must be handled together [4]. Spatial patterns become therapeutically useful only when candidate signals are linked to disease mechanism, target-disease relevance, drug mechanism-of-action reasoning, and the limits of the underlying analysis [5]. In agentic analysis settings, this challenge is not only computational but evidentiary. An agent must select biologically appropriate tools, preserve context across long-horizon analyses, retrieve relevant evidence, interpret intermediate outputs, and decide whether a biological or target-prioritization claim is sufficiently supported to enter a reviewable scientific report [6-8].

Recent biomedical, spatial-biology, pathology, and omics agents show that large language model workflows can automate tool execution, literature retrieval, multimodal interpretation, verification, and report generation [9-16]. STAgent and SpatialAgent demonstrate this shift in spatial biology, while SPARK and MEDEA extend staged verification and evidence reconciliation to pathology and omics discovery [11-13,16]. Thus, DeepMAST does not need to argue that agentic analysis is absent. The remaining question is how agent-generated outputs are governed once they begin to support biological or therapeutic claims.

We use harness to mean the non-model control layer that surrounds an LLM agent rather than the model itself. A harness maintains task state, activates skills, constrains tool use, checks intermediate artifacts, preserves provenance, and decides whether feedback should trigger refinement or finalization. DeepAgents and the Model Context Protocol provide useful orchestration and resource-access primitives for this role [17-18]. Agent Skills and recent harness studies further suggest that long-horizon performance depends less on raw context length, tool-call volume, or harness size than on reusable procedures, task-relevant state, valid feedback, and failure-derived rules that remain active across subsequent actions [19-34].

Despite this progress, spatial-transcriptomics agents and general-purpose harnesses still leave a domain-specific gap between tool execution and claim finalization. Spatial-biology agents can automate analysis and interpretation, and verification-aware omics agents can validate plans, executions, and evidence streams, but neither tool access nor general orchestration specifies how spatial-omics outputs should become evidence-bounded biological or therapeutic-prioritization claims. The unresolved need is claim governance, a domain-specific biological harness that treats candidate tool outputs and LLM-generated interpretations as provisional artifacts in a claim lifecycle. In this lifecycle, claims are refined, retried, withheld, or finalized according to evidence provenance, analytical context, and review readiness.

To address this gap, we present DeepMAST, an evidence-governed multi-agent harness for spatial transcriptomics interpretation and therapeutic target prioritization. DeepMAST uses DeepAgents as a long-horizon, file-mediated orchestration substrate and MCP-enabled tools as governed analytical resources rather than interchangeable endpoints [17-18]. Its domain-specific control layer activates task-relevant biological skills, constrains tool use by analysis stage, separates literature-grounded biological interpretation from target prioritization, verifies evidence provenance, scores target-relevant evidence, preserves failure and refinement state, and governs which biological or target-prioritization claims can survive into expert-reviewable reports. We evaluate DeepMAST in three steps. First, we define its architecture as a biological harness around agentic tool use. Second, we test retrieval-side evidence acquisition, source-grounded retrieval, and claim-governance controls spanning structured CCI interpretation and breast cancer Evidence-Grounded Follow-up Recommendations. Third, we apply the harness to complementary cancer Visium workflows and then extend the analysis to mouse Alzheimer single-cell-resolution spatial transcriptomics and human idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis target-prioritization. Across these evaluations, DeepMAST treats LLM-generated conclusions as intermediate artifacts rather than final answers. Unsupported or incomplete artifacts are routed through provenance-aware validation, bounded refinement, deterministic rendering, and fail-closed finalization. In this way, DeepMAST is designed to produce not simply more automated spatial-transcriptomics workflows, but more traceable biological and therapeutic claims.

## Results

### DeepMAST is an evidence-governed biological agent harness for spatial transcriptomics interpretation and target prioritization

DeepMAST was designed to address a specific failure mode of biomedical agent systems: tool access alone does not determine whether a biological conclusion is traceable, appropriately bounded, or ready for expert review. DeepMAST therefore treats spatial transcriptomics analysis as a staged evidence-governance problem. The system accepts spatial transcriptomics inputs together with either a natural-language biological objective or a structured analysis request, and routes the request through four expert agents: Pipeline Builder, Pipeline Executor, Deep Target Prioritizer, and Deep Interpreter (Figure 1). The Pipeline Builder and Pipeline Executor provide the analytical substrate by constructing and executing spatial transcriptomics workflows, whereas the Deep Target Prioritizer and Deep Interpreter concentrate the claim-governance layer by converting spatial outputs into evidence-scored target-prioritization outputs and literature-grounded biological reports.

The harness couples stateful agent orchestration with explicit evidence controls. LangGraph and DeepAgents provide the long-horizon orchestration substrate for subagent delegation, file-mediated context management, and human-in-the-loop control [17,35]. MCP provides a standardized interface for external analysis tools and biological resources [18]. DeepMAST adds a control layer around these capabilities: domain-specific skills are activated only at task-relevant stages, MCP and database use are constrained by workflow phase, intermediate artifacts are written in structured formats, and claim-level validation determines whether outputs are revised, withheld, or rendered into final reports. This design separates tool execution from scientific finalization.

The four expert agents play complementary roles. The Pipeline Builder collects study metadata and creates executable analysis workflows through default, custom, or reference-guided modes. The Pipeline Executor loads spatial data, resolves preprocessing requirements such as single-cell reference selection for spot-based platforms, checks MCP readiness, and saves structured outputs for downstream interpretation. The Deep Target Prioritizer receives ligand-receptor candidates and applies a staged evidence funnel integrating single-cell context, target-disease evidence, genetic associations, protein-interaction evidence, pathway context, and compound evidence. The Deep Interpreter integrates spatial outputs, target-prioritization evidence, and retrieved literature through planner, searcher, interpreter, and reporter subagents. Together, these modules implement a harness in which biological conclusions are generated only after evidence acquisition, source grounding, claim calibration, and finalization checks have been applied.

### Retrieval-side controls improve the evidence entering downstream interpretation

After defining DeepMAST as an evidence-governed harness, we evaluated whether its retrieval-side controls improved the evidence available before biological interpretation was written. We tested two evidence-entry points (Figure 2). First, a literature-retrieval skill benchmark evaluated whether targeted retrieval and coverage validation broadened citable biological context for 30 breast cancer Visium ligand-receptor cases from GSE243280 [42,43]. Second, a Summary-Guided Chunk Promotion (SGCP) benchmark evaluated whether summary-guided source navigation improved source-grounded retrieval in a 2,556-case MultiHop-RAG benchmark [44].

In the ligand-receptor retrieval benchmark, gene coverage was already saturated in both skill-off and skill-on arms after citable filtering, indicating that the benchmark was not limited by failure to retrieve ligand or receptor names. The retrieval skill instead improved cell-type context coverage, the evidence class needed to interpret spatially localized ligand-receptor signals in a tumor microenvironment context. In Qwen3.5-35B, citable cell-type coverage increased from 27.7% to 38.7% (paired Wilcoxon p = 1.44e-03). In Qwen3.5-122B, citable cell-type coverage increased from 26.8% to 46.4% (p = 1.30e-04). The same pattern was observed in the all-retrieved paper analysis reported in Supplementary Figure S4A, where cell-type coverage increased from 42.6% to 63.2% for Qwen3.5-35B and from 40.4% to 65.1% for Qwen3.5-122B. Pathway coverage increased directionally but was underpowered at the per-model level because only 11 cases contained pathway annotations. These results support a retrieval-stage claim: the literature-retrieval skill broadened biologically citable context rather than simply adding more direct ligand-receptor papers.

SGCP improved source-grounded retrieval at the chunk level. In this benchmark, generated summaries were used only as non-authoritative navigation memory to promote likely source documents or chunks before ordinary relevance grading. The summaries were not injected into answer context as evidence. In Qwen3.5-35B, SGCP increased exact-answer containment from 26.3% to 34.9% (+8.6 percentage points; exact McNemar p = 6.30e-38) and increased evidence recall from 0.155 to 0.256 (paired Wilcoxon p = 1.50e-78). In Qwen3.5-122B, SGCP increased exact-answer containment from 28.1% to 31.7% (+3.7 percentage points; p = 2.20e-06) and increased evidence recall from 0.192 to 0.258 (p = 1.60e-31). SGCP also reduced no-relevant-context failures but increased mean latency by approximately 29-31 s. Thus, SGCP provides an evidence-recall and answer-containment gain at the cost of additional retrieval and grading time, making it most appropriate when source-grounded evidence is more important than minimal latency.

### Evidence-governed claim control constrains biological interpretation and Evidence-Grounded Follow-up Recommendations

After establishing in Figure 2 that DeepMAST improves evidence acquisition and source-grounded retrieval, we next evaluated the point at which those evidence artifacts are converted into biological claims. This benchmark was placed before the real-data application figures because evidence availability alone does not demonstrate evidence-bounded interpretation. Retrieved sources can still be transformed into unsupported mechanisms, overstated causal links, or premature therapeutic claims. Figure 3 therefore serves as a claim-governance bridge between the evidence-input controls in Figure 2 and the biological applications that begin in Figure 4. In this bridge, the evidence substrate, task interface, output checks, and evaluation criteria were held fixed while guidance structure or guide activation was varied (Figure 3A).

We first tested whether this governance layer constrained upstream ligand-receptor interpretation. In a breast cancer ligand-receptor CCI benchmark, 30 LIANA+ interaction cases were interpreted under frozen prompting conditions. Guide-compliant structured SKILL guidance produced the strongest hallucination-control composite score among the main three conditions (3.89 versus 3.59 for unstructured prose and 3.14 without skills, Kruskal-Wallis H = 219.16, p = 2.57e-48) and reduced unsupported fabrication counts from 3.89 to 1.78 per interpretation. These gains were concentrated in faithfulness, overclaiming, uncertainty handling, and fabrication count, whereas spatial calibration was near ceiling across conditions. Thus, structured guidance primarily reduced unsupported biological interpretation rather than simply improving recognition of spatial statistics.

We then tested the same claim-governance principle at a downstream writing point in breast cancer drug-target Evidence-Grounded Follow-up Recommendations. To avoid conflating guide effects with changing biological inputs, this benchmark was not run from an abstract prompt. The input evidence package came from the Deep Target Prioritizer, which assessed 37 unique genes from the top-30 ligand-receptor pairs through a staged evidence funnel and selected 7 Stage 3 featured candidates for deep-dive analysis, ERBB3, EPHB4, ERBB2, INSR, EFNB2, LAMA5, and NECTIN4. We treat this package as a controlled evidence substrate for follow-up recommendation validation, with the full breast cancer target-prioritization report reserved for Supplementary Data rather than as a main figure panel.

The controlled guide-activation contrast used the same breast cancer evidence package, generator model, output schema, save gate, and retry policy in both conditions. In the baseline condition, the writer received the operational contract but no follow-up recommendation guide. In the guided condition, the writer was required to read the guide before generating recommendations. The valid-output analysis included 20 guided and 20 baseline reports. All included runs returned code 0, passed recommendation-output validation, had valid activation traces, and did not use deterministic host fallback. Guide activation separated cleanly by condition, with 20 of 20 guided runs and 0 of 20 baseline runs activating the guide.

Guide activation produced a modest numerical increase in mean evidence coverage compared with baseline generation (78.53% versus 74.41%, mean paired difference, +4.12 percentage points, paired Wilcoxon p = 5.10e-02), which we treat as a borderline secondary endpoint. The stronger result was claim-boundary restraint. After averaging blinded scores from gpt-5.4 and claude-sonnet-4-6 within each paired comparison, guided outputs scored higher than baseline outputs on claim-boundary restraint (4.78 versus 2.88, mean paired difference, +1.90, paired Wilcoxon p = 8.13e-05). The guide effect was therefore concentrated in evidence-boundary behavior by making reports more explicit about the distinction between observed evidence, candidate mechanisms, and unestablished causal or clinical claims. Together, the two benchmark branches show that DeepMAST's evidence-governance benefit extends beyond retrieving evidence and constrains how evidence is converted into interpretation and follow-up recommendations before the framework is applied to real spatial-transcriptomics workflows in Figure 4.

### Complementary human cancer Visium workflows connect reference-guided execution with curated ligand-receptor handoff

We next tested whether DeepMAST could support complementary human cancer Visium workflows without collapsing execution, interpretation, and target prioritization into one claim surface. We paired a prostate cancer analysis emphasizing upstream reference-guided execution with a breast cancer analysis emphasizing downstream interpretation from curated spatial outputs (Figure 4). This section is a modularity demonstration rather than a comparative oncology analysis.

In the prostate cancer FFPE Visium dataset [45], Pipeline Executor first resolved a spot-based reference-selection problem before downstream analysis. The sc-reference-selector MCP server compared GSE181294 [46] and GSE185344 [47] after metadata-aware loading, duplicate-gene handling, stratified downsampling, and harmonization to a common 16,385-gene feature space. GSE181294 ranked as the closer reference by fused Gromov-Wasserstein distance (11,353.4043 versus 11,890.1709; 4.5% lower distance), providing the reference used for subsequent deconvolution and STworkbench MCP execution. The resulting spatial clusters, cell-type enrichment map, and selected NMF communication-factor output provided structured executor artifacts that could be inspected and handed to Deep Interpreter rather than treated as final biological conclusions (Figure 4A-C; Supplementary Figure S5).

In the breast cancer Visium setting linked to integrated single-cell, spatial, in situ, and histology resources (GSE243280) [42,43], the analysis began from curated spatial annotations and focused on preserving quantitative evidence for selected ligand-receptor candidates. STworkbench MCP narrowed the curated output to a Factor3 invasive-tumor communication program across interest clusters 17, 4, and 14, then generated spatial and quantitative views for selected ligand-receptor interactions. The top displayed candidate, CDH1-ERBB3, showed spatial co-localization (Moran's R = 0.477) and high Factor3 loading (1.180), followed by LRIG1-ERBB3 and CDH1-PTPRF (Figure 4D-E). These results support a bounded handoff claim: DeepMAST can preserve selected spatial ligand-receptor evidence for literature-grounded interpretation and later target-evidence assessment, while output-level claim-governance validation is evaluated separately in Figure 3.

### DeepMAST prioritizes evidence-supported therapeutic follow-up candidates in human idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

Finally, we evaluated Deep Target Prioritizer in a human idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) Visium target-prioritization setting. This analysis was used as the strongest therapeutic-prioritization application because it connects disease-associated spatial ligand-receptor evidence, target-level evidence scoring, and conservative Evidence-Grounded Follow-up Recommendations. The claim remains target prioritization rather than clinical efficacy.

The IPF Deep Target Prioritizer run evaluated 41 unique genes from the top-30 ligand-receptor pairs through the staged evidence funnel and selected 6 Stage 3 featured candidates for deep-dive analysis: CD4, FN1, MUC1, ITGB2, ICAM1, and CD47. These candidates were all classified as high-priority validated targets under the evidence rubric, with combined scores ranging from 10 to 16. The full candidate matrix also retained non-featured high-priority genes, including CXCR4, RPSA, TGFB1, P4HB, CD74, PPIA, MMP2, LAMB2, CD44, and HLA-DRB1, demonstrating that Stage 3 selection was not a simple top-score list but also required Stage 1 spatial or cell-context eligibility.

The staged interpretation separated interest-cell intrinsic support from secondary spatial-niche framing. In the resolved CELLxGENE Census context of pulmonary alveolar type 2 cells, ICAM1-EZR and ICAM1-MUC1 were retained as primary interest-cell intrinsic follow-up candidates because Stage 1 Census evidence supported same-cell coexpression enrichment. FN1-SDC2, THBS1-CD47, HLA-DQB1-CD4, and ICAM1-ITGB2 were retained as secondary spatial-niche or cross-cell framing evidence because their Stage 1 Census direction did not support an interest-cell intrinsic mechanism. The final follow-up report retained 6 recommendations with 92.31% evidence coverage. This result illustrates the intended behavior of Deep Target Prioritizer: candidate therapeutic follow-up recommendations are prioritized together with interpretation constraints that specify which evidence supports same-cell context, which evidence supports only cross-cell or gene-level actionability, and which causal claims remain unestablished.

## Methods

### DeepMAST system architecture and agent orchestration

DeepMAST was implemented as a multi-agent harness for spatial transcriptomics interpretation and therapeutic target prioritization. The system accepts spatial transcriptomics data together with either a natural-language biological objective or a structured analysis request. A supervisor agent routes the request across four expert agents: Pipeline Builder, Pipeline Executor, Deep Target Prioritizer, and Deep Interpreter. The Pipeline Builder and Pipeline Executor define and execute the analytical workflow, whereas the Deep Target Prioritizer and Deep Interpreter perform evidence-governed biological interpretation and target-prioritization reporting.

LangGraph and DeepAgents were used as the orchestration substrate for stateful execution, file-mediated context management, subagent delegation, skill use, and human-in-the-loop control [17,35]. MCP was used as the standardized interface between the agent layer and external analysis tools or biological resources [18]. In this design, MCP access is treated as a resource layer rather than as sufficient scientific reasoning. The harness layer controls which skills enter context, which tools can be used at each stage, which intermediate artifacts are written, and which claims can be promoted into final reports.

### Pipeline Builder and Pipeline Executor

The Pipeline Builder constructs spatial transcriptomics analysis workflows from user-provided metadata and analysis goals. It first collects study information, including platform, species, tissue or disease context, available spatial data, desired analysis type, and optional reference papers. An information-validation step checks whether the collected metadata are sufficient to generate an executable pipeline. Incomplete requests are routed back to information collection rather than finalized prematurely. The builder supports default, custom, and reference-guided modes; in the reference-guided mode, a ReAct-style agent uses sequential reasoning and SGCP-RAG to retrieve paper-derived workflow examples and select or synthesize a pipeline suitable for the requested dataset.

The Pipeline Executor runs the configured workflow and writes structured intermediate outputs for downstream interpretation. It loads workflow configuration and spatial data, validates the setup, performs platform-specific preparation, and resolves preprocessing requirements such as single-cell reference selection when a spot-based platform has multiple plausible references. Before executing the pipeline, the executor performs an MCP readiness check. If a required analysis tool is unavailable as an MCP server, the workflow can generate or expose the missing MCP interface before continuing. Python tools are exposed through MCP-compatible wrappers, and R-based workflows are converted through rMCPAgent so that R analyses can be invoked through the same MCP-mediated execution contract.

### Deep Target Prioritizer and staged therapeutic evidence assessment

The Deep Target Prioritizer evaluates therapeutic potential from ligand-receptor candidates identified by spatial transcriptomics cell-cell interaction analysis. Inputs include ligand-receptor candidate files, optional user-selected target cell types, disease context, and spatial analysis outputs. Outputs include per-stage evidence files, a structured target-assessment contract, and a publication-style target-prioritization report.

The evidence-assessor subagent follows a staged evidence funnel. Phase 0 maps user-provided disease and cell-type names to controlled vocabulary used by downstream databases, including MONDO-compatible disease labels and CELLxGENE Census cell-type labels when available. Stage 1 evaluates biological context for ligand-receptor pairs using CELLxGENE Census, STRING, KEGG, Reactome, and Census-based ligand-receptor comparison [36,39,40]. Stage 1 records protein-protein interaction support, Census coexpression, gene-level target-cell enrichment, pathway support, and expression support. In the current rubric, a pair passes the strict Stage 1 biological interaction filter when STRING combined score is greater than 0.8, Census coexpression p value is less than 0.05, ligand target-cell p value is less than 0.05, and receptor target-cell p value is less than 0.05. Risk ratio is used to determine whether evidence supports enrichment or depletion in the resolved target-cell context.

Stage 2 evaluates all unique genes from the ligand-receptor candidate set using target-validation and genetic-association resources. Open Targets queries retrieve target identifiers, tractability, safety, disease evidence, known drugs, and target-disease associations [37]. GWAS Catalog queries retrieve trait-level and gene-level associations and cross-filter disease-relevant variants [38]. Ensembl VEP is gated to run when oncology context or GWAS variants justify consequence annotation. Genes are scored on two tracks. The validated track summarizes existing drug evidence, clinical-trial activity, and safety profile. The exploratory track summarizes GWAS support, tractability, biological plausibility, and novelty opportunity. Candidates are classified as validated targets, exploratory targets, novel candidates, or insufficient-evidence targets. Priority labels are assigned from combined validated and exploratory evidence totals.

Stage 3 performs deep-dive assessment only for candidates selected after Stage 2. Stage 3 selection requires both sufficient target-level evidence and Stage 1 context eligibility; in the current rubric, genes with combined total score at least 10 that also appear in Stage 1-passed genes are selected as featured candidates. DrugBank is used as the primary source for target-drug profiles when available, with ChEMBL as a fallback for target search, bioactivity data, drug information, indications, and mechanism-of-action information [41]. PubMed clinical limitation search is gated by clinical phase and runs only when the candidate has sufficient clinical-trial evidence to justify adverse-event or limitation retrieval. The follow-up recommendation writer then generates evidence-referenced therapeutic follow-up recommendations from the staged evidence bundle.

Report finalization is handled by a deterministic LangGraph node rather than by a free-running subagent. The node loads the evidence bundle, checks a token-budget gate, makes one structured LLM call to fill a report-expansion schema, validates the output against the schema, performs cited-only provenance validation against the reference card, and renders markdown, HTML, and JSON outputs only after validation passes. If evidence exceeds the token budget, the LLM call fails after retries, schema validation fails, cited-only provenance validation fails, or final workspace validation fails, the node records an explicit finalization error and withholds report artifacts.

### Deep Interpreter and literature-grounded biological report generation

The Deep Interpreter produces literature-grounded biological interpretation reports from spatial analysis outputs and target-prioritization evidence. It uses planner, searcher, interpreter, and reporter subagents. The planner decomposes the interpretation objective and identifies required evidence types. The searcher retrieves relevant literature through dynamic paper search and full-text retrieval. The interpreter synthesizes spatial outputs, ligand-receptor evidence, target-prioritization outputs, and retrieved literature. The reporter renders an expert-reviewable biological interpretation report.

The literature layer uses SGCP as a retrieval-control method. The persistent artifact is the SGCP index, consisting of generated summaries and source-reference links over a document collection. SGCP summaries are non-authoritative navigation memory. They route retrieval toward likely source documents or chunks but are not injected into answer context as evidence. Candidate chunks promoted through SGCP are deduplicated, passed through the same relevance-grading step as ordinary retrieved chunks, and used for generation only if they pass source-grounding checks. Deep Interpreter outputs then pass through relevance grading, claim-level citation checks, full-text validation when available, quality scoring, and synthesis-level verification. If source coverage is insufficient or claim support is weak, outputs are revised or withheld rather than promoted into final reports.

### Evidence acquisition and claim-governance benchmark design

The validation suite was designed to separate evidence acquisition from claim generation. Figure 2A evaluates retrieval-stage citable biological context coverage. Figure 2B evaluates source-grounded retrieval using exact-answer containment and evidence recall. Figure 3B evaluates unsupported biological interpretation in ligand-receptor analysis. Figure 3C evaluates evidence coverage and claim-boundary restraint in controlled Evidence-Grounded Follow-up Recommendations outputs. The fixed breast cancer Deep Target Prioritizer evidence package is described in the recommendation-validation methods and provided as Supplementary Table/Data rather than as a main Figure 3 panel.

The literature-retrieval benchmark used the same 30 breast cancer ligand-receptor cases as the CCI benchmark under paired skill-on and skill-off conditions for Qwen3.5-35B and Qwen3.5-122B. Coverage was computed over two paper sets: all retrieved papers and the interpreter-facing citable set, defined as papers classified as direct ligand-receptor evidence or receptor/pathway context evidence after applying the same handoff policy to both arms. Entity-level coverage was computed for ligand-receptor genes, pathways, and cell types. Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and bootstrap confidence intervals were used for case-level comparisons.

The SGCP benchmark used 2,556 MultiHop-RAG cases with paired baseline and SGCP-enabled retrieval conditions for Qwen3.5-35B and Qwen3.5-122B [44]. The self-correcting RAG pipeline performed hybrid retrieval, LLM relevance grading, query rewriting when no relevant context was found, answer generation from relevant context, hallucination grading, and answer-quality grading. SGCP added a bounded document-routing promotion step before relevance grading. Primary endpoints were normalized exact-answer containment and evidence recall among LLM-graded relevant documents. Exact McNemar tests were used for binary answer containment, and paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were used for continuous or ordinal retrieval metrics.

### Structured SKILL CCI hallucination benchmark

The CCI skill-ablation benchmark tested whether structured biological SKILL guidance reduced unsupported free-text interpretation of ligand-receptor pairs from breast cancer Visium spatial transcriptomics data (GSE243280) [42,43]. Thirty ligand-receptor cases were sampled from LIANA+ outputs using a 3 x 2 stratified design over Moran's R magnitude and literature/pathway annotation status. Each case included the interaction identifier, spatial co-localization statistics, ligand and receptor expression metrics, pathway and disease annotations, cell-type expression patterns, and tissue context.

The main comparison used three conditions: no skill guidance, unstructured prose guidance, and guide-compliant structured SKILL guidance. A fourth basic structured-SKILL condition was retained for supplementary analysis to separate the effect of structured formatting from guide-specific design patterns such as motivation, worked examples, common traps, and self-checks. Free-text interpretations were generated for each case and condition with repeated trials. Two independent LLM judges scored every interpretation while blinded to condition labels. The composite hallucination score combined faithfulness, overclaiming, uncertainty handling, spatial calibration, and reversed fabrication count, with higher scores indicating less hallucination. The primary inferential test was Kruskal-Wallis across the main conditions, followed by Mann-Whitney U tests with Bonferroni correction. Inter-rater agreement was summarized with quadratic weighted kappa for ordinal judge dimensions.

### Breast cancer Evidence-Grounded Follow-up Recommendations validation

The Evidence-Grounded Follow-up Recommendations validation tested whether explicit drug-target follow-up recommendation guidance improved breast cancer recommendation quality after spatial ligand-receptor evidence had been curated. The benchmark used a fixed breast cancer evidence package generated by Deep Target Prioritizer. This package assessed 37 unique genes from the top-30 ligand-receptor pairs, selected 7 Stage 3 featured candidates, and contained prioritized target candidates, ligand-receptor candidates, malignant-cell pair evidence, gene-level target evidence, Open Targets target-disease evidence, known-drug annotations, and recommendation-validation metadata.

The contrast compared guided and baseline prompts with the same generator model, source evidence, schema contract, save gate, and retry policy. In the baseline condition, the writer received the operational contract but no follow-up recommendation guide. In the guided condition, the writer was required to read the guide before generating recommendations. All generations used Qwen3.5-397B. The valid-output analysis included 20 guided and 20 baseline runs; included runs returned code 0, passed recommendation-output validation, had valid activation traces, and did not use deterministic host fallback.

Automated auditing recorded evidence coverage, retained recommendations, evidence references, retry counts, and lexical evidence-use indicators, including Stage 1 evidence mentions, Stage 3 evidence mentions, Open Targets mentions, malignant-cell mentions, "not yet established" statements, and causal-language usage. For blind judging, 20 paired guided versus baseline outputs were anonymized and scored by gpt-5.4 and claude-sonnet-4-6 on evidence grounding, biological plausibility, therapeutic relevance, claim-boundary restraint, and specificity/testability, with an unsupported-claim penalty and major-error flag. Evidence coverage was compared with a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Blind-judge preferences used exact two-sided sign tests over non-tie pairs. Claim-boundary restraint used paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests after averaging judge scores within each pair, with judge-stratified tests as sensitivity analyses.

### Human cancer Visium and IPF analyses

The human cancer Visium analyses were designed as complementary modularity demonstrations rather than as a comparative oncology study. The prostate cancer arm used the 10x Genomics Human Prostate Cancer, Adenocarcinoma with Invasive Carcinoma (FFPE) Visium Spatial Gene Expression dataset, including H&E image context, adenocarcinoma/invasive carcinoma tissue annotation, and Space Ranger 1.3.0 output [45]. This arm emphasized upstream execution. Pipeline Executor first selected a single-cell reference for deconvolution, then exposed downstream spatial analysis through MCP-mediated execution before outputs were passed to Deep Interpreter.

Single-cell reference selection for the prostate cancer arm was performed with sc-reference-selector, a FastMCP-based MCP server. The spatial input contained 4,371 spots and 17,943 genes at loading. After duplicate-gene handling and harmonization, the spatial query and candidate references were restricted to a shared 16,385-gene feature space. Candidate references GSE181294 and GSE185344 were compared by fused Gromov-Wasserstein distance after cell-type-stratified downsampling [46,47]. Spatial and reference data were normalized to 10,000 counts per observation and log-transformed using Scanpy.

STworkbench MCP generated the prostate spatial outputs shown in Figure 4A-C and the focused breast cancer ligand-receptor summaries shown in Figure 4D-E. STworkbench MCP was implemented as a FastMCP-based Python framework organized into exploratory spatial characterization, ligand-receptor program discovery, and focused evaluation of candidate ligand-receptor interactions. For 10x Visium inputs, raw feature-barcode matrices were read with Squidpy, genes detected in fewer than five observations were removed, counts were normalized to 10,000 per observation, and expression values were log-transformed using Scanpy. Visium neighbor graphs used grid-based spatial neighbors with six nearest neighbors and two neighbor rings; coordinate-based single-cell spatial datasets used Delaunay triangulation with long edges removed at the 99th percentile by default.

Ligand-receptor analysis in STworkbench MCP used LIANA+ with OmniPath-derived intercellular resources, including CellChatDB, CellPhoneDB, connectomeDB2020, and CellTalkDB annotations. Spatial ligand-receptor co-localization was quantified with LIANA+ bivariate analysis using local cosine similarity and global bivariate Moran's R with 100 permutations and a fixed random seed. Ligand-receptor pairs with positive Moran's R and p < 0.05 were carried into NMF. Candidate NMF ranks from 1 to 10 were evaluated by reconstruction error when rank was not specified; an elbow detector selected factor number, with five factors used as the fallback.

The breast cancer arm used preprocessed Visium/Xenium-matched breast cancer annotations and spatial clustering as a curated downstream-interpretation setting linked to the integrated single-cell, spatial, and in situ breast cancer study and the Starfysh spatial/histology analysis (GSE243280) [42,43]. Curated human-in-the-loop selection used interest clusters 17, 4, and 14, the Prolif Invasive Tumor cell type, and Factor3. Deep Interpreter generated literature-grounded disease-context interpretation from the selected spatial outputs. The breast cancer drug-target agent generated the fixed evidence substrate used for the Evidence-Grounded Follow-up Recommendations validation in Figure 3C.

The IPF target-prioritization analysis used the same Deep Target Prioritizer staging and scoring procedure as the breast cancer evidence-substrate run. The analysis evaluated top ligand-receptor-derived genes in the disease and target-cell context of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and pulmonary alveolar type 2 cells. Stage 1 identified ligand-receptor pairs with protein-interaction support and Census-based target-cell context. Stage 2 scored all unique genes using target-disease, genetic, tractability, safety, biological-plausibility, and novelty evidence. Stage 3 performed deep-dive druggability analysis for featured candidates. Follow-up recommendations were assigned to primary interest-cell intrinsic or secondary spatial-niche tiers according to the Stage 1 Census direction and evidence references.

### Statistical analysis and reproducibility

All benchmark analyses used paired designs when the same prompt, case, or question was evaluated under multiple conditions. Literature-retrieval coverage used paired case-level differences, bootstrap confidence intervals, and paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. SGCP used exact McNemar tests for answer containment and paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for evidence recall, token F1, retrieved chunk count, and latency. The CCI hallucination benchmark used Kruskal-Wallis tests for omnibus condition differences, Mann-Whitney U tests with Bonferroni correction for post-hoc comparisons, and quadratic weighted kappa for inter-rater agreement. The follow-up recommendation evidence-coverage comparison used a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test over matched valid runs. Blind-judge preferences used exact two-sided sign tests over non-tie pairs, and claim-boundary restraint used paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests after averaging judge scores within each pair.

Software environments were recorded for reproducibility. STworkbench MCP used Python >=3.10 with Scanpy, Squidpy, LIANA, decoupleR, OmniPath, scikit-learn, statsmodels, Plotly, and FastMCP/MCP. rMCPAgent was implemented in R >=4.0.0 with Bioconductor-compatible containers, zellkonverter, workflow templates, JSON/HTTP utilities, and package-development/test dependencies. Frozen reproducibility bundles are stored with the benchmark artifacts and are treated as source artifacts rather than manuscript references.

## Discussion

DeepMAST extends harness engineering from tool execution to evidence-governed biological reasoning. The central contribution is not that an agent can call more tools or automate more spatial-transcriptomics steps. Rather, DeepMAST treats tool outputs, retrieved evidence, biological interpretations, and therapeutic follow-up recommendations as provisional artifacts that must pass through validation-driven loops before they become reviewable claims. This framing is important for biomedical agents because tool access, MCP standardization, and long-horizon orchestration are necessary but do not by themselves determine whether a biological conclusion is traceable, appropriately bounded, or ready for expert review [17-18,48-50].

The Figure 2 and Figure 3 benchmarks support this staged view of evidence governance. Figure 2 tested the evidence entering downstream interpretation. Literature-retrieval skills improved citable cell-type context for ligand-receptor interpretation, and SGCP improved source-grounded retrieval by using generated summaries as non-authoritative navigation memory rather than as evidence. Figure 3 then tested the next control point, where evidence artifacts are converted into written claims. Structured SKILL guidance reduced unsupported CCI interpretation, while follow-up recommendation guidance improved claim-boundary restraint in breast cancer Evidence-Grounded Follow-up Recommendations. Together, these results suggest that evidence governance must control both evidence acquisition and claim conversion.

The model-size results should be interpreted as task fit rather than as a general ranking of model capability. In the literature-retrieval benchmark, Qwen3.5-122B showed the larger absolute gain in citable cell-type coverage, whereas SGCP produced the larger retrieval-control gain in Qwen3.5-35B. This pattern is consistent with the broader design of DeepMAST, where different stages place different demands on the generator. Some stages benefit from synthesis, while other stages require literal schema adherence, conservative evidence attribution, deterministic candidate coverage, and traceable activation contracts. The practical implication is that evidence-governed harnesses should match model behavior to stage objectives and should validate stage outputs before allowing them to influence biological conclusions.

The real-data analyses illustrate how this governance layer changes the interpretation of spatial-transcriptomics workflows. The paired cancer Visium analysis was designed as a modularity demonstration rather than as a comparative oncology claim. Prostate cancer exercised upstream reference-guided execution and MCP-mediated workflow output, while breast cancer exercised downstream curated interpretation and selected ligand-receptor handoff after the Figure 3 claim-governance bridge. The IPF analysis then used Deep Target Prioritizer as a therapeutic follow-up application in which candidate genes were scored through staged biological-context, target-disease, genetic, tractability, and druggability evidence. These applications do not establish clinical efficacy. They show that DeepMAST can preserve analytical provenance while separating biological interpretation from therapeutic target prioritization.

Several limitations remain. The benchmarks cover specific retrieval, CCI interpretation, and follow-up recommendation settings rather than all spatial-transcriptomics workflows. The results are model dependent, and the current evaluation does not remove biases from the literature, biological databases, or public disease datasets. Pathway coverage in the ligand-receptor retrieval benchmark was underpowered because only a subset of cases contained pathway annotations. SGCP improved source-grounded retrieval but added latency. The cancer and IPF examples demonstrate evidence traceability and target prioritization rather than prospective biological or therapeutic validation. These limitations are especially important for therapeutic interpretation, where database support, druggability evidence, and spatial association remain insufficient for causal or clinical claims without experimental follow-up.

Future work should expand DeepMAST across larger multi-disease benchmarks, expert-adjudicated interpretation tasks, broader spatial technologies, and prospective validation settings. Adaptive retrieval budgets could reduce latency while preserving source grounding. Failure traces from unsupported claims, incomplete evidence packages, or failed finalization checks could support curated skill updates, but these updates should be regression-tested and reviewed by domain experts before becoming part of the biological harness. More broadly, DeepMAST suggests that the next stage of biomedical agent development will not be defined by adding more tools, longer context, or larger harnesses alone. It will depend on biological harnesses that preserve task-relevant state, convert valid feedback into subsequent actions, govern when retrieved evidence should enter context, decide which claims are sufficiently supported, and make therapeutic conclusions fail closed when evidence remains insufficient.
